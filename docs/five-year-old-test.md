# Why we made it this way

## The four big ideas

### 1. Nobody is the boss

1.1 Nobody here is the boss. Not one person. Not one grown-up. Everybody is the same size.

1.2 Anybody can make their own list of who to believe. Every list counts just as much as every other list. The list that comes in the box is only the first one we put in there. You can put it away and use your own. [HARD]

1.3 Friends find each other by showing a note they wrote and put their own name on. They do not have to ask a big store or a big phone book first. [HARD]

1.4 You cannot buy people trusting you. You earn it by helping. Helping gets you something like a sticker. Stickers are very hard to fake, and you cannot hand your stickers to anybody else. They stay yours because you did the helping. [HARD]

1.5 There is no special boss screen and no plain little-kid screen. Everybody sees the same screens.

1.6 What is different is what you can show. Some kids are holding a note that says "this one is mine to see." Some kids are not.

1.7 So we never grey out a button and say "you are not big enough for this." That tells a lie about who is bigger.

1.8 The true thing to say is about where the thing is going and why. Not about who matters more.

### 2. A secret is not hiding. A secret is telling the right person.

2.1 A secret is not a thing you shove in a hole. A secret is a thing that goes to the right person and does not go to the wrong person.

2.2 Every place in your life has its own rules about what you tell. What you say at home is not what you say on the playground.

2.3 Before anything about you moves from one place to another, five things have to be said out loud. Each one gets a name written on it, like when you sign your drawing.

2.4 One: who is it about?

2.5 Two: who is doing the telling?

2.6 Three: who is getting told?

2.7 Four: what kind of thing is it? A scraped knee? A drawing? A snack?

2.8 Five: what was the promise about where it may go next? [HARD]

2.9 The doctor may know your tummy hurts. That follows the rules of the doctor place.

2.10 That same doctor may not sell it to a stranger. That breaks the rules. It is still wrong even if you once signed a paper that said okay.

2.11 So every screen has to be able to answer one question: "which rule let me see this?"

2.12 That is why everything on the screen wears a name tag saying which group it belongs to. The group tells you what kind of thing it is, and that is one of the five things.

2.13 Show a number with no name tag and you dropped one of the five. Then nobody can tell if it should be there at all.

### 3. Two kids can look at the same screen and see different things, and both are right

3.1 Some things belong only to your family. When a thing is family-only, we do not even put up a sign about it.

3.2 Somebody outside cannot go get it. They cannot read it. They cannot even find out it is there.

3.3 Being able to see something is not a little switch next to your name that a boss flips on.

3.4 It depends on which notes you are holding. A note is a small message with a name written on it, and anybody can check that the name is really really that person's. [HARD]

3.5 So you and your friend can look at the same screen and see different things. Both of you are right. Nobody is being left out, and nobody is in trouble.

3.6 Now the big one. An empty spot and a hidden spot look the same. They are NOT the same. This is the thing grown-ups get wrong when they make the screen prettier.

3.7 "Nothing here yet" means: come put something here!

3.8 "This one is not yours to know about" means: do not even make the kid wonder. Do not leave an empty shape where the thing would go.

3.9 The machine works very hard to never mention it. The screen must not go and blab it.

### 4. Six promises, and no promise gets to beat another promise

4.1 Here is what we want. A world that keeps working. A world where all the different creatures who can feel things get to grow, be happy, be treated fair, and still think things are amazing. [HARD]

4.2 The six promises are: be kind. Do not hurt. Be honest. Say true things even when you are not sure. Let people choose for themselves. Be fair.

4.3 No promise gets to beat another promise. You cannot say "I was being kind" as your reason for hurting somebody.

4.4 If two promises bump into each other and nobody can work it out, the machine stops and asks a grown-up it trusts. It does not just pick by itself.

4.5 Some screens look like boring tools for the people who built the thing. They are not boring. They are promises you can look at. You may not tidy them away.

4.6 The pages that show what happened and what the machine did: that is "be honest." Anybody may look and check the thinking.

4.7 The page where you take your yes back: that is "let people choose." A yes has to be something you can un-say. So it gets its own big page. It does not hide in a tiny line in the settings.

4.8 The safety page sits near the top of the list because "do not hurt" comes first. We did not stick it on at the end.

4.9 Even a "something went wrong" message is a promise. Telling the truth when you are not sure is one of the six. That is why every single screen owes us four different faces.

## The six rules

### 1. Do not make bosses

1.1 No boss screen. No levels. No "be a bigger kid to see this."

1.2 Everybody being the same size is how the thing is built. It is not a switch you can flip.

### 2. Never show a thing without its name tag

2.1 The group a thing belongs to tells you what kind of thing it is. That is one of the five things you must say.

2.2 A number with no group is a thing wandering around with no name on it. Nobody can tell if it should be there.

### 3. Empty and hidden are not the same

3.1 Empty says: put something here!

3.2 Hidden must never say there is anything there at all.

3.3 Same blank spot on the screen. Opposite jobs.

### 4. Four faces for every screen, every time

4.1 Full. Empty. Still coming. Broke.

4.2 "Broke" has to LOOK different from "empty." Not the same grey nothing.

4.3 One of the six promises says tell the truth when you are not sure. So this is a must, not a nice extra.

### 5. Put things together by who they are for, not by what they do

5.1 There are five circles: you and your helper, your family, the people near you, the big groups far away, and everybody in the whole world.

5.2 Each circle has its own rules about what may be told and who may be told it. They are not a list of toys to pick from. [HARD]

5.3 If you squashed all the circles into one, you would rub out the whole idea.

### 6. You can always take your yes back, so show the door

6.1 Any screen that asks for your things, or passes your things on to somebody else, has to show you the way out where you say "no more."

6.2 The way out has to be easy to find. And the machine has to understand it too, not just people. [HARD]
